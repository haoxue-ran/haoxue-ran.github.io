<h3 style="color: #0f172a; font-size: 1.25em; font-weight: 700; margin-top: 20px; margin-bottom: 12px;">
  <i class="fas fa-file-alt" style="color: #2563eb; margin-right: 6px;"></i> Abstract
</h3>

<p style="text-align: justify; font-size: 1.02rem; line-height: 1.7; color: #334155; margin-bottom: 35px;">
Fenton pretreatment effectively mitigates organic membrane fouling via synergistic oxidation and coagulation. However, its key drivers and intrinsic mitigation mechanisms remain unknown. Here, a progressive data-driven framework integrating machine learning (ML), Shapley additive explanations (SHAP), and causal inference methods across three extensive datasets (>6,700 samples) was developed to quantitatively elucidate Fenton-induced fouling mitigation from macro- to microscopic levels. The developed ML models exhibited exceptional prediction performance across all datasets (R<sup>2</sup><sub>adj</sub> = 0.804–0.976). The results of SHAP analysis revealed that Fenton pretreatment largely governed fouling mitigation (44.1%). Specifically, Fe<sup>2+</sup> was 1.8 times more critical than H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> was, and both were significantly nonlinearly correlated with fouling mitigation. By capturing these nonlinear dose–response relationships, we identified the optimal trace Fenton dosage levels (Fe<sup>2+</sup> = 0.1 mM; H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> = 0.075 mM) tailored for specific organic foulants (sodium alginate). Microscopically, causal inference revealed the underlying mitigation mechanisms: notably, oxidation-driven electrostatic repulsion (51.7%) contributed four times more than the coagulation-driven size exclusion effect did (12.8%). This enhanced electrostatic repulsion primarily weakened foulant–foulant intermolecular cohesion (57.6%), which far outweighed the reduction in foulant–membrane adhesion (29.6%). Ultimately, architectural restructuring of the fouling layer (72.7%) driven by these attenuated interfacial interactions effectively mitigated membrane fouling, decisively outweighing the contribution from the reduction in the deposited foulant mass (27.3%). In this study, we not only quantitatively decouple the mechanistic pathways underlying Fenton-mitigated membrane fouling but also provide a versatile and transferable data-driven paradigm for elucidating complex membrane fouling phenomena.
</p>
